Executive summary

This project began with a practical question: could an Ecuador network derived from PyPSA-Earth be made coherent enough to run? The answer was not a single successful command. It was a sequence of repairs, source reconciliations, failed paths, and increasingly explicit boundaries between official evidence, modelling assumptions, generated products, and legacy experiments.

The work moved from a 2017/2018 notebook history toward a 2022 SNI baseline. It now has a checksum-aware artifact contract, an explainable notebook sequence, source-traceable generation and planning tables, and an annual solve that returned ok / optimal. But the central scientific acceptance gate remains the gate: the modeled annual supply must reconcile explicitly and agree with the fixed CENACE carrier reference. The Phase 09 record does not justify calling the network validated.

A LinkedIn-ready summary

I’ve been documenting the development of an Ecuador power-system model built with PyPSA and PyPSA-Earth-derived inputs.

The interesting part was not simply getting an optimization to run. It was reconstructing what the model meant: repairing a network topology, mapping generation and demand to Ecuador-specific evidence, separating the historical 2017 planning experiments from the current 2022 baseline, and making every assumption visible enough for someone else to inspect.

The project now has a staged, notebook-led workflow, source-traceable generation and Master Plan tables, an explicit CENACE 2022 comparison contract, and a documented student handover. An annual solve can terminate optimally, but the broader validation gate is deliberately still open while energy-boundary residuals and carrier comparisons are resolved.

That distinction—between a model that runs, a result that looks plausible, and a model that is scientifically validated—is the main lesson I’m carrying forward.

The development story

Seven turns in the work

Starting point · 2017–2018 evidence

First, make the inherited model run

The legacy chain contains demand forecasts, generation matching, network checks, renewable estimation, pruning, consistency checks, and scenario-building logic. Its strongest execution evidence is the nearly completed no-pruning consistency notebook; the broader pruned notebook ends with a saved KeyError. That is useful forensic evidence, not a clean production workflow.

The practical barrier

Runtime was also a data problem

PyPSA-Earth-derived work depended on large upstream artifacts: a base grid and renewable profiles, with a much larger ERA5/atlite cutout relevant only to legacy regeneration. Environment discovery, notebook kernels, API changes, hidden generated files, and limited documentation made reproducibility part of the scientific task. The later manifest and Zenodo handoff turn those implicit prerequisites into explicit checks.

Ecuador-specific reconstruction

A network is not Ecuador just because it has Ecuadorian names

Manual work included snapping non-standard voltage groups, pruning orphan elements, retaining reviewed 138 kV corridors, repairing transformer parameters, attaching 251 loads and 133 generators, and recording plant and load connection evidence. Location quality is mixed: exact matches, manual canton assignments, and province-level proxies are kept distinct rather than flattened into one certainty.

The planning context changed

2017 scenarios became legacy; 2022 became the baseline

Nuclear scenarios and other future additions had a historical role in exploration, but they are not official Master Plan facts. The refactor quarantines those records and rebuilds the 2022 planning interface from source-cited generation and grid tables. Published timing is retained separately from later teaching delays and scenario assumptions.

The annual model

From snapshots to an accountable year

The current contract fixes the mainland SNI boundary, 8,760 hourly timestamps, demand controls of 27,561.62 GWh and 4,388.06 MW peak, and a carrier reference drawn from CENACE. Domestic supply, imports, exports, storage, losses, curtailment, load shedding, and the residual must be visible in one energy balance.

Exploration after the baseline

Future scenarios are a separate layer

Renewable screening, Master Plan expansion filtering, El Niño / drought experiments, and nuclear sensitivities are useful questions only when their assumptions are named. A hydro profile proxy can reveal dispatch sensitivity; it cannot by itself establish historical hydrology or prove a future portfolio is feasible.

Phase 09 handover

Refactoring became teaching infrastructure

The target is not a black-box script. It is a sequence a student can run, inspect, restart, and extend: preflight, demand, generation, profiles, topology, assembly, annual validation, planning, scenario construction, and comparison. The repository is closer to that goal, while retaining a list of evidence gaps instead of hiding them.

Methods and limits

Running is necessary; it is not sufficient

The annual solve used HiGHS and returned ok / optimal after a large simplex run in the Phase 06B evidence. The validation contract, however, is deliberately broader than solver status. It requires compatible accounting boundaries, carrier mapping, explicit energy closure, low load shedding, recorded provenance, and all governing screens.

Open scientific issue. The reports retain an unresolved 931.43 GWh source-boundary residual. The contract reports total-energy error but does not turn that residual into guessed losses. The 5% carrier rule is not weakened, and no failing carrier is silently omitted.

Demand is controlled to official annual and monthly CENACE values, but its hourly shape and bus shares come from a 2013 PyPSA-Earth template. Renewable availability also uses 2013 weather-derived profiles, while hydro profiles are normalized proxy series with reviewed assignments and confidence labels. These choices make the workflow reproducible; they do not recreate 2022 weather or plant telemetry.

Beyond the baseline

Scenarios with their assumptions attached

Future work screens renewable generation, Master Plan projects, delayed teaching schedules, and stress cases. The supported planning interface preserves all cited rows, coordinates and timing evidence where available, and flags unknown physical definitions. It does not turn a list of projects into a runnable expanded grid by inventing buses, endpoints, or capacities.

Nuclear appears only as a quarantined historical or student-defined axis. It is excluded from the official baseline and Master Plan tables. The same separation applies to drought: a stress test can ask “what if hydro availability is lower?” without claiming that its synthetic profile is a measured drought year.

What remains

A handover is an honest boundary

Supported now

Source registers, preflight rules, demand controls, normalized generation inputs, explicit topology repairs, planning evidence, scenario schemas, reports, figures, and a sequential notebook map are documented and testable.

Still unresolved

The full fresh-user walkthrough and complete annual scientific acceptance remain the decisive work. Large generated networks and solved outputs are not treated as canonical merely because they exist locally. Phase 10 still has to prune only after useful provenance is migrated.

The durable result is therefore methodological: a model that tells its next user where it came from, what it assumes, which experiment produced a figure, and which claim it is not yet entitled to make.
